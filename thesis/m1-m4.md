# M1–M4. Corpus, chunking, statistics, and retrieval

## M1. Corpus construction

**Source**. The corpus is the CIAM Terminale C series (Collection Inter-Africaine de
Mathématiques, ed. Saliou Touré), the official Ivorian Terminale C textbook. Ten non-geometry
chapters were indexed alongside two official BAC série C papers (2022, 2024), for twelve
documents (full list, data card §2.2); geometry is excluded. The BAC série C 2025
paper was held out from indexing: nine of its items appear in the evaluation set, so
quarantining it prevents the retriever from surfacing a gold-standard solution as a retrieved
passage.

**OCR and cleaning**. Scanned PDFs were converted to text-plus-LaTeX with `marker` [Paruchuri,
2024], chosen for being locally executable, open-source, and structure-preserving (headings and
inline-LaTeX equations), supporting both the content-type taxonomy (§M2) and equation-aware
metrics. Validation on one hand-transcribed chapter gives prose CER 11.51% / equation exact-match
87.46% (full figures, Annex E), typical for equation-dense textbook OCR, and the precondition for
the downstream generation and faithfulness part in §M6; the single-chapter scope makes it indicative, not definitive.
The marker model presented degeneration in multiple independent runs of the OCR. Thus, post-processing dropped figure regions and excised vision-model repetition loops, leaving a sentinel in their place. Six syntactic checks then set a per-chunk hallucination flag: five heuristics for likely OCR damage, plus the sentinel test, whose presence is proof rather than suspicion that content was lost. Flagged chunks are retained but tracked, so a downstream failure on one is distinguishable from a genuine retrieval failure.
(retained but tracked: rates in
§M3.1; `src/ocr/clean.py` and `src/corpus/filters.py`, data card §4–5)

## M2. Chunking and the content-type taxonomy

The cleaned corpus was segmented based on a specific structure organised in units. So, each chunk is one of the 8 typographic units: theory
block, theorem, proof, worked example, definition, exercise, solution, and remark. There was no uniformed size / length cap for each chunk, meaning that even two different theory chunks could have different sizes. 
Also, empty-body headings are dropped. After all that segmentation of the clean corpus, the corpus obtained consisted of 722 chunks.
Each carries exactly one of eight labels assigned by a deterministic keyword heuristic over marker's content heading (and not topic heading) text. The code to identify the content heading and assign the label can be seen in 
(`src/corpus/chunking.py`). One limitation is
load-bearing for H2: some worked examples are not titled "examples" in the book. Hence they are usually silently labelled
`theory`, contaminating the theory bucket. This was sorted to some extent, as can be seen in §M3.3 and §M5.5. 

## M3. Corpus statistics, evaluation set, and limitations

### M3.1 Corpus statistics

722 chunks; 424,010 prose characters; 75,853 words; 5,313 equations; 15.7% of chunks carry ≥ 1
dropped figure region and 14.7% a hallucination flag. Per content type: theory 185 (25.6%), theorem
142 (19.7%), example 141 (19.5%), remark 82 (11.4%), proof 57 (7.9%), definition 48 (6.6%), exercise
34 (4.7%), solution 33 (4.6%). The pedagogical retriever's (§M5.2) bucket aggregates are thus 514
theory-bucket (theory + theorem + definition + proof + remark), 174 example-bucket (example + solution), and 34 excluded `exercise` (≈ 3:1). The chunks labelled "exercises" were excluded because they do not have any solution given in the book.  Per-chapter composition is
in Annex D.

### M3.2 Evaluation query set
This is to evaluate the retriever. It consists of:
100 queries, stratified across the ten chapters and three structural categories — 60 single-leçon,
20 multi-notion, 20 hard-edge. By source, 91 synthesised / 9 BAC-2025: the 9 BAC-2025 queries
come from the de-indexed 2025 paper and give an ecologically valid slice of authentic problems,
while the synthesised queries supply stratified coverage and the multi-notion/hard-edge density that
BAC items alone cannot (authoring protocol, §M7).

### M3.3 Tagging-quality estimates

Tagging quality rests on two targeted audits (data card). A precision check: of the 24 worked
examples recovered from `theory`-labelled chunks by the imperative/study cues, all 24 were genuine worked
content (zero false sweeps). A recall check surfaces the dominant residual error: a manual audit of
n = 40 `theory`-labelled chunks (seed 42) estimates ≈ 32% are in fact headingless worked examples
(95% CI 20–48%). The dominant tagging error is therefore one-directional `example`→`theory` leakage
of unmarked content, not cross-class confusion; its H2 consequences are carried in §M5.5 and
full-corpus quantification is future work (§M9).

### M3.4 Geometry exclusion

Geometry chapters and geometry queries are excluded: geometry is densely figure-dependent, so indexing figure-stripped geometry chunks would yield text-only passages with systematically lower retrievability and pedagogical utility than analytic content, confounding any cross-chapter comparison. A figure-preserving multimodal pipeline is the cleaner long-term solution and is future work (§6.3).

### M3.5 Other limitations

Figure-dropping (15.7%) means a failure on a figure-dependent passage cannot be cleanly attributed to a retriever or generator defect; hallucination-flagged chunks (14.7%) are tracked separately so a failure on them is distinguishable; all retrieval experiments report the 91 synthesised and 9 BAC-2025 queries separately, so any difference between authored and authentic queries is visible rather than averaged away; and generalisation to third-party Terminale C textbooks is not directly supported
(single-textbook corpus).

## M4 Retrieval: 

### M4.1 BM25, dense, and the RRF hybrid

The stack for the retrieval has three channels:  a keyword-matching, a semantic-matching, and a mix of both  
- Sparse BM25 [Robertson & Zaragoza, 2009] over a French-tokenised corpus
- Dense multilingual-E5 [Wang et al., 2024] (1024-dim, documented French coverage) ranked by cosine similarity.
- RRF hybrid [Cormack et al., 2009] fusing the two. The hybrid is the baseline against which the pedagogical retriever (§M5) is contrasted under H2; hybrid-vs-dense is the registered test of H1 (§M4.4).   

The hybrid (and the later explained pedagogical retriever in M5) consume the same RRF-fused pool with each of BM25 and dense contributing to its top-20, re-ranked by $\text{rrf}(d)=\sum_{r} 1/(60+\text{rank}_r(d))$. Its top-5 in fused order is the hybrid's output.

The maths-motivated BM25 tokenisation (single-character-variable preservation, accent folding, no lemmatisation), the E5 `query:`/`passage:` prefix convention, and the fusion parameters are detailed in Annex M.E.

### M4.2 H1 evaluation protocol and status

H1 tests whether the RRF hybrid outperforms dense-only on the 100-query set against the human-panel qrels (§M7). Three metric families are reported at $k \in \{5, 10\}$ — Recall@$k$ (binary, ≥ 2), nDCG@$k$ (graded 0–3), MRR — with primary metric nDCG@5, paired-bootstrap significance
(§M9), and secondary analyses by source (leakage check) and query type. The retrieval
infrastructure is built; the full (query, chunk) relevance package of 1,565 pairs was dispatched,
returned, and processed into qrels.  
The H1 results are reported in Chapter 4 and state that the hybrid's advantage over dense comes from the keyword channel, and it holds only on the 91 synthesised queries while the ordering reverses for the 9 authentic BAC items. Because the synthesised queries were written in the textbook's own vocabulary, part of that advantage may be an artefact of how they were constructed rather than a property of the retriever.
