# M5. Pedagogical retrieval
This relates to Hypothesis 2.
## M5.1 Motivation

The retrievers of §M4 are content-type-blind: they rank by relevance signals that have no constraint on what kind of content (theory, or definition, example, etc) is returned. For a Terminale C student, this approach might be judged irrelevant because they will need the information given to them to be structured. Solving a typical problem requires both the underlying machinery (a definition,
theorem, or method) and an example showing it applied. This has been documented as the worked-example effect
[Sweller, 1988; Atkinson et al., 2000] and is mirrored in CIAM (the offficial curriculum texbook) itself, where each leçon interleaves
exposition with "exercices résolus". A blind retriever's top-k may be almost all theory or almost all examples; either failure mode degrades tutoring (theory alone leaves no applied
template; examples alone leave no formal scaffolding to generalise).

The pedagogical retriever is therefore a selection policy that enforces a content-type mix at retrieval time: a fixed number of theory passages and a fixed
number of example passages, both drawn from the same RRF-fused pool the hybrid baseline uses.


## M5.2 Design choices

For slot allocation of that selection policy, the eight-value taxonomy used in the CIAM textbook collapses into two buckets and one
excluded category:

| Bucket   | Constituent labels                            |
|----------|-----------------------------------------------|
| Theory   | `theory, theorem, definition, proof, remark`  |
| Example  | `example, solution`                           |
| Excluded | `exercise`                                    |

The theory bucket aggregates content that establishes formal claims or supplies motivating
commentary; the example bucket aggregates content that show worked examples internal to a lesson (`example`) and the textbook's own `solution`s. `exercise` in the textbook are unsolved problems, with no guidance on how to solve them. Therefore, it has been deliberately excluded from both: a problem statement carries no instructional content.

Hence, the corpus segmentation for the pedogical retriever is: 514 theory-bucket, 174
example-bucket, and 34 excluded (≈ 3:1). 

The 3-theory : 2-example split is fixed for every experiment and is methodological, not tuned
on a development set. Tuning $(k_\text{theory}, k_\text{example})$ on held-out data would
convert H2 from a hypothesis test into a hyperparameter search, inviting multiple-comparisons
concerns across the grid and, more substantively, answering the wrong question. The meaningful
question is not whether some split outperforms the hybrid baseline but whether the principle
of enforced theory–example mixing does at all. Fixing the split at a defensible a priori value
(three theory passages, in approximate proportion to the dominant theory-bucket share) tests
the principle without incidental-tuning noise. Treating the split as a tunable hyperparameter
and reporting the resulting Pareto frontier is future work (§6.3).

As a fallback, it was decided that if the
pool holds fewer than three theory-bucket or two example-bucket passages, backfill the unfilled
slots with the highest-ranked remaining passages of either bucket, recording a per-query
fallback flag used in §M5.5 to condition the H2 contrast on example-availability.


## M5.3 The slot-allocation Algorithm

For each query, the pedagogical retriever proceeds in three steps. 

(1) Pool construction with no re-ranking beyond RRF:
the same RRF fusion used by the hybrid baseline of §M4 (each of BM25 and dense contributes its
top-20; fused by $\text{rrf}(d) = \sum_{r} 1/(60 + \text{rank}_r(d))$ [Cormack et al., 2009]). 

(2) Slot filling: traverse the fused list in descending
order, assigning the first three theory-bucket passages to the theory slots and the first two
example-bucket passages to the example slots, skipping `exercise`. 

(3) Fallback: if the
pool holds fewer than three theory-bucket or two example-bucket passages, backfill the unfilled
slots with the highest-ranked remaining passages of either bucket, recording a per-query
fallback flag used in §M5.5 to condition the H2 contrast on example-availability. 

The five-passage payload is numbered `[1], …, [5]` in fused-rank order (preserving rank for the
generator's citation behaviour, §M6). Production code is `src/retrieval/instructional.py` (instructional = pedagogical retriever)and here is a reduced
reference version:

```python
def pedagogical_retrieve(query, k_theory=3, k_example=2):
    pool = rrf_fuse(bm25.top(query, n=20), dense.top(query, n=20), k=60)
    theory, example, leftover = [], [], []
    for chunk in pool:                            # descending RRF score
        if chunk.type in THEORY_BUCKET and len(theory) < k_theory:
            theory.append(chunk)
        elif chunk.type in EXAMPLE_BUCKET and len(example) < k_example:
            example.append(chunk)
        elif chunk.type != "exercise":
            leftover.append(chunk)
    fallback = (len(theory) < k_theory) or (len(example) < k_example)
    while len(theory) + len(example) < (k_theory + k_example) and leftover:
        theory.append(leftover.pop(0))            # backfill, either bucket
    return theory + example, fallback
```

## M5.4 How H2 is tested

H2 is therefore a hypothesis about selection, not ranking: baseline and pedagogical retriever
see an identical fused pool and differ only in which five passages they select at fixed top-k
= 5. In fact, there are 5 passages that would populate the generator's context window (§M6), although the H2 test reported in this thesis is purely retrieval-side and invokes no generator.
Because the pedagogical retriever consumes the same RRF-fused pool as the hybrid baseline and
returns the same number of passages, the H2 comparison focuses on the selection policy: any
difference at fixed top-5 is attributable to which five passages are selected from a shared
pool, not to tokenisation, embedding, fusion parameters, or pool size.

The pedagogical retriever is evaluated on the retrieval side against the human-panel qrels
(§M7) under rubric v1.0. The primary retrieval-side metric is the per-query nDCG@5 difference
between the pedagogical and hybrid retrievers over the 100-query set, with paired-bootstrap
significance (§M9). Crucially, nDCG@5 is neither necessary nor sufficient for the H2
pedagogical-quality claim: the retriever deliberately passes over higher-relevance theory
chunks to seat the example slots, so it can lose on nDCG@5 and still tutor better, or win with
no tutoring gain. The retrieval-side contrast therefore tests only whether the enforced mix is
achieved without sacrificing relevance; the pedagogical-quality verdict itself is pending
the generation-side evaluation (§M10). The infrastructure is built and was validated
end-to-end on the 1,565-pair package; populated results include the per-query nDCG@5 and Recall@5
differences with bootstrap CIs, the example-slot fallback rate, and the
example-availability-conditioned slice.

## M5.5 Limitations

Two limitations are flagged here and revisited under threats to validity (§M9).

Baseline contamination from the theory bucket. A non-trivial fraction of `theory`-labelled
chunks are in fact headingless worked examples — content demonstrating application that lacked
the typographic marker the tagger keys on. A manual audit (n = 40 chunks read by hand, seed 42,
2026-06-10; data card §5.6) estimated ≈ 32% of `theory`-labelled chunks are headingless worked
examples (95% CI 20–48%), about 60 chunks or 12% of the 514-chunk theory bucket (the other four theory labels were not audited); a full-corpus estimate is future work. To that extent, the hybrid
baseline can already surface worked-example content through the theory channel, partially
closing the gap the pedagogical retriever enforces. The H2 contrast could therefore be conservatively
biased: any positive effect appears in spite of a baseline that captures, by chance, some of
what the pedagogical retriever imposes by construction.

For the example-poor queries and the fallback regime, the fallback rate is reported
with the H2 results. H2 is therefore reported in two slices: a marginal
slice over all queries (the realistic estimate of operational impact) and a conditional
slice restricted to queries that triggered no example-slot fallback (the cleaner test of the
principle).
